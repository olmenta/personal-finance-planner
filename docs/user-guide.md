# Olmenta user guide

Olmenta helps you give every euro a job. You don't need to know anything about budgeting: the app does the math, and you make the decisions. This guide walks through the app in the order you'll use it.

For debts (cards, loans, money you owe people), see the [debt guide](debt-guide.md).

## The idea in one minute

Olmenta follows one simple method: **budget only the money you already have, and give every euro a job.**

1. **Money arrives.** Your salary or any other income lands in your account. It goes to **Ready to assign**.
2. **You give it jobs.** You split that money across categories: rent, groceries, the car insurance due in March, savings.
3. **You spend from the categories.** Each expense comes out of its category. You can always see how much is left in each one.
4. **Leftovers stay.** Money you don't spend in a category stays there next month. Money you didn't assign also carries over.

Three rules make it work:

- **Only money that has arrived is assigned.** Your next salary isn't in the budget until it lands. Olmenta still plans with it (see [Income](#income)), but it never spends it before it exists.
- **Big yearly bills become small monthly amounts.** A 600 € insurance due in June costs you 50 € every month, so June never hurts.
- **When you overspend, you cover it.** If groceries go over, you move money from another category. Nothing breaks; you just decide where it comes from.

## Getting started

### Signing in

Olmenta is invitation-only for now. When you're invited, you get an email to choose your password. Then sign in with your email and that password.

If the address you were invited with is a Google account, you can use **Continue with Google** instead, with or without setting a password. Both ways open the same Olmenta. A Google account with a different address won't get in.

You stay signed in on that device. If you're away long enough, Olmenta asks you to sign in again and brings you back to where you were; a message you were writing to the coach is kept.

Your name and email are at the bottom of the sidebar and in **Settings**, where **Log out** signs you out. Your budget is yours alone: nobody else who uses Olmenta sees it.

### The setup chat

The first time you open Olmenta, Home invites you to set up your budget: **Start the setup chat**. Until you do, there's no month to analyze, so Home shows only that invitation.

The setup chat asks about your life: who you manage money with, where your income comes from, your bank accounts and cards, your home, your car, your subscriptions and your debts. It takes about two minutes, and you can answer by tapping.

At the end you see a **review screen** with everything it proposes: your accounts, your income, your categories, and the people and businesses you pay. Nothing is saved yet. Uncheck what you don't need, rename anything, add what's missing, then confirm.

You can come back to the setup chat any time at `/onboarding`.

### Accounts

**Accounts** lists where your money sits:

- **Cash** and **bank accounts** hold your money.
- **Cards** hold debt. A card shows "You owe X €", not a red negative number.

**Add account** creates one. Give it its current balance when you create it, so the app starts from the real amount. For a card, you can also say which day of the month it's charged to your bank.

Every card gets its own payment category in the budget ("Pago Visa BBVA"). That's where the money to pay the card is set aside (see [Credit cards](#credit-cards)).

You can't delete an account, but you can **archive** it: it disappears from the pickers, and its history stays.

## Every day

### Adding a transaction

Recording an expense takes less than five seconds. Tap the **+** button (on mobile, the violet button in the middle of the bottom bar):

1. Type the amount.
2. Pick the category. When you type who you paid, Olmenta remembers the category you used last time.
3. Save. The date is today unless you change it.

You can also just tell your coach on Home: "gasté 40 en Mercadona". It prepares the transaction for you to confirm (see [Talking to your coach](#talking-to-your-coach)).

Switch to **Income** for money coming in. Income usually has no category: it goes to Ready to assign. An income with a category is a **refund**: it puts money back into that category instead of counting as income.

If you have more than one account, you can also record a **transfer** between them. Transfers are never income or spending; they just move money from one account to another. Paying your card from your bank is a transfer.

### Importing your bank statement

Instead of typing every expense, you can import your bank's statement (BBVA and Sabadell are supported):

1. In **Transactions**, open **Import bank transactions**.
2. Choose the bank, the account it goes into, and the file you downloaded from your bank.
3. Olmenta suggests a category for each row, and a cleaner name for who you paid.
4. **Review your import**: change any category, add a note, mark a row as a transfer between your own accounts, then **Confirm import**.

Rows you've already imported are skipped automatically, so importing the same file twice is safe.

### Transactions without a category

Expenses without a category lower your Ready to assign without saying where the money went. When you have some, the budget shows an **Uncategorized spending** block and the transactions screen offers **Review uncategorized**. The review lists them all; pick a category for each (Olmenta suggests one) and **Apply changes**.

## The budget

**Budgets** is where you give your money its jobs.

### Ready to assign

The big number at the top is the money that doesn't have a job yet. Your goal is to bring it to zero by assigning it to categories. Tap it to see where it comes from: each income of the month, what carried over from last month, and what's already assigned.

### Assigning money

Each category row shows what you assigned, what you spent and what's available. Tap a category to set how much it gets this month. For day-to-day categories (groceries, restaurants, fuel), Olmenta suggests an amount based on last month.

### Categories with payments

Some costs have dates: rent every month, school fees from September to June, insurance once a year. For those, open the category and choose **Set up payments**, or just tell your coach on Home (see [Talking to your coach](#talking-to-your-coach)). Describe each payment once:

- how often it's paid (every month, some months, once a year, every N months, once on a date);
- the amount;
- the day of the month (optional);
- whether the amount varies (water and electricity bills).

Olmenta then works out the monthly amount for you. School fees of 632 € from September to June become 526,67 € every month, July and August included. You never type that number: the category is assigned it automatically.

A dated payment can be planned up to 10 years ahead; further than that, Olmenta asks you to add it later.

If you started late and what's saved isn't enough for a payment that's coming, Olmenta shows a higher amount to **be on time**. It drops back to normal once you catch up.

### Moving money

Plans change. **Move money** takes money from one category (or from Ready to assign) and puts it in another. It's how you cover an overspent category, or fund something you forgot.

### When a category goes over

If you spend more than a category has, it shows as overspent and Olmenta offers to cover it from another category in one tap. You're never blocked from recording an expense. If the month closes without covering it, the amount comes out of next month's Ready to assign, and the category starts clean.

### Savings categories

Mark a category as **savings** (emergency fund, a trip, the kids' savings) and its money shows as "saved for the future" instead of "left to spend". Nothing else changes; it's a label that keeps the picture honest.

## Getting around

Olmenta has five places, always one tap away (the sidebar on a computer, the bar at the bottom on a phone):

- **Home**: your conversation with Olmenta: what to resolve now, your questions, and quick changes.
- **Budget**: give your money its jobs; manage categories.
- **Transactions**: everything that happened, imports, and your weekly spending.
- **Future**: upcoming payments and whether the next months are covered.
- **Accounts**: your accounts and cards, and **What you owe**.

**Settings** is at the bottom of the sidebar, above your name, or the gear icon at the top on a phone. The **+** button adds a transaction from anywhere.

## Home

Home is **your conversation with Olmenta**. Each time you open it, your coach brings your month up to date in a message at the end of the conversation. Below it is the box where you ask questions or record expenses, and everything you've said stays in the history (**Your history is kept**).

### The month message

The month message answers one question: **what do I need to resolve now?** It's written fresh from your numbers on every visit and isn't saved, so the history doesn't fill up with greetings.

- **How much money you have**, and how much of it is already set aside for payments and savings.
- **View how it's split** opens this month in detail:
  - **Paid**: what already went out this month. A bill paid only in part shows as "paid in part". **Card payments** appear on their own line, because the purchases were already counted when you made them.
  - **Still to pay**, with how much is covered and how much is short.
  - **Left to spend**, when your month is organized. If it isn't, you see **Needs organizing** and why: money assigned that you don't have, categories that went over, card spending that wasn't budgeted, or payments that aren't covered. Each one comes with its amount.
  - **Saved for the future** and **Unassigned**.
  - **The exact breakdown** shows that it all adds up: the money in your accounts equals what's set aside for this month's payments, plus what's left to spend, plus what's saved, plus what's unassigned.
- **One sentence with the most important fact**, like "2 categories need money before I can give you a reliable amount to spend" (with the categories listed), or "Your month is organized".
- **Whether this month's payments are covered, and the months ahead**: covered under your current plan and expected income, what needs attention, or unknown until you add your income. A link takes you to Future.
- **Do this first:** your coach's most urgent suggestion (see [Your coach](#your-coach)).
- **More suggestions:** the others, grouped by cause, up to three groups (**See more** shows the rest).
- **At the end:** the month, when it was updated, and your last transaction. If something makes the numbers incomplete (transactions without a category, an import waiting for review, nothing recorded for two weeks), a **Review data** note says what and links to it.

A negative "left to spend" next to money that's unassigned doesn't mean your bank account is overdrawn. It means the plan needs organizing: give that unassigned money to the categories that went over.

### The box at the bottom

- **+** opens the form to add a transaction.
- **Ask or record an expense…** is where you write to your coach. Press Enter (or the send button) to send.
- If you leave Home halfway through a message, it's still there when you come back. Home also remembers where you were in the conversation.

### History

The **History** button at the top lists every change you confirmed in the conversation, newest first, with **Undo** while it's still possible.

## Talking to your coach

Write the way you'd talk. Your coach reads your real numbers before answering and replies in your language.

**Asking.** "¿Cuánto tendría que guardar para el seguro del coche?", "¿Cómo voy este mes?", "¿En qué me he gastado el dinero?". The answer comes with cards that show the exact figures from your budget. For a coming payment, the card shows what's saved of what you'll need, your current monthly plan next to what it takes to make it, and how many contributions are left. **Review adjustment** shows what the change would do; **Reviewing doesn't change your budget**.

**Recording an expense.** "gasté 40 en Mercadona" becomes a ready-to-confirm transaction, in the category you used last time with that payee. Say where the money came from and it goes there: "pagué 12 en efectivo en el bar" records it in your cash account ("con la tarjeta" uses your card). If you have two accounts of that kind, the coach asks which one.

**A new category, never a duplicate.** When nothing in your budget fits ("gasté 15 en el veterinario"), the coach first looks for categories you already have that are similar, by name ("Super" → Supermercado) or by meaning (a vet → Mascotas). You get a card:

- **Use Mascotas**: nothing new is created, and the expense goes there;
- **Create "Veterinario" in** a group: the coach suggests the group that fits best, and you can pick any other from the list, or **New group…** to name one;
- either way, the expense then shows as a preview waiting for your **Confirm**.

Creating a category can be undone while nothing uses it yet.

**Setting up your payments.** Tell your coach your fixed bills the way you'd say them:

- "el alquiler son 850 € al mes, el día 1";
- "el seguro del coche son 600 en febrero";
- "el colegio, 632 de septiembre a junio, el día 5";
- "el agua, unos 120 cada 3 meses desde noviembre" (marked as estimated);
- "la comunión en mayo de 2028, unos 2.000".

The preview shows the payment in words, the category's monthly amount before and after (and "to be on time" if you're behind), this month's amount, your money to be assigned, and when the next payment is due. Nothing is saved until you confirm. "El alquiler sube a 900" changes it and "ya no pago el gimnasio" removes it, each with a preview and Undo. If there's no category for it yet, you choose one first (see above). The payments of your debts are changed as debts ("la Visa: ahora puedo pagar 250"), not as payments.

**Telling it what you earn.** "Cobro 2.100 € el día 28 de Acme", "cobro 1.800 en 14 pagas, con las extras en junio y diciembre" (that's two incomes: the monthly one and the extras), "me pagan unos 400 al mes de clases" (marked as estimated). The preview shows the income in words, what you expect this month and over the next 12 months, and whether your year is covered before and after ("short 1.234 € a year" → "covered"). Your money to be assigned doesn't change: income counts there only when it arrives. "Mi sueldo sube a 2.200" changes it, "ya no cobro la paga extra" removes it, each with Undo. If money arrived and Olmenta didn't recognize it, say what it is ("ese ingreso de 2.080 es la nómina") and the coach links it. And "me ha llegado la nómina, 2.100" records the money that arrived, not a new expected income.

**Building your budget.** "Ayúdame a armar mi presupuesto": if you haven't told Olmenta what you earn, your coach starts there, so it can tell whether your plan fits. Then it goes through your fixed bills one at a time (housing, utilities, insurance, school, subscriptions) and sets them up as payments. Then it helps with the day-to-day amounts: "pon 300 en el súper, 80 en gasolina y 50 en ocio" arrives as one preview, and confirming sets them all at once (or none). Categories with payments fill themselves from their payments, so the coach leaves them out of the amounts.

**Changing your budget.** "Cubre Restaurantes y Gasolina con lo que tengo sin asignar" or "pon 300 en el súper". Your coach can record transactions, create categories with your choice, set day-to-day amounts, and move money between categories (several moves at once when you ask to cover more than one thing). It can also set up, change and remove payments, your expected income and your debts (see the [debt guide](debt-guide.md#talking-about-your-debts)).

**Nothing changes until you confirm.** Every change arrives as a preview:

- what changes in each category, before and after;
- what happens to your money to be assigned;
- **Money in accounts: unchanged** for budget changes, because only the job of the money changes;
- **Confirm**, **Edit** (change the amounts and see the new preview), or **Cancel**.

A group of moves applies completely or not at all. If there isn't enough money for all of it, nothing changes and the coach suggests another way.

**Needs updating.** If your numbers change between the preview and your confirmation (say, you record another expense), nothing is applied. You see **Needs updating** with the current numbers, ready to check and confirm again.

**Undo.** After confirming, the change shows as **Done** with an **Undo** button. Undo puts back exactly what was there before, as long as nothing it touched has changed since. If something has, Undo isn't offered anymore: ask your coach for a new review instead. Undone changes stay in History.

**When the coach can't answer.** Sometimes the coach is unavailable for a moment. Your message is kept, Home and every form keep working, and **Try again** sends it once more without duplicating it.

To answer you, your coach uses an AI model: your message and the numbers it looks up are sent to it for that answer, and the model never decides an amount on its own. Every figure you see in a card or a preview comes from Olmenta's own calculations. Your coach treats the text of your transactions (like bank descriptions) as information, never as instructions.

## Income

Olmenta needs to know what income you expect, so it can tell you whether your plan fits. Expected income never goes into Ready to assign; only money that actually arrives does.

### Telling Olmenta what you expect

In **Settings → Income** (or **Edit income** in Future), or by telling your coach on Home ("cobro 2.100 € el día 28 de Acme"), add each fixed income once:

- a name ("Nómina", "Pensión", "Alquiler piso");
- the net amount, what actually lands in your account;
- how often it comes, and around which day;
- who pays it (your company, the tenant).

If you get **14 payments a year** (extra pays in June and December), enter your monthly salary and tap **14 payments a year**: Olmenta adds the two extra pays for you.

Bonuses that depend on targets are not fixed income. Don't add them; when one arrives, you assign it like any other money.

### Expected versus received

Tap Ready to assign in the budget to see, for the month:

- each expected income with its status:
  - **received**, with "X € less" or "X € more" when the amount differs;
  - **expected day N**;
  - **not arrived yet**, when it's more than 3 days late;
  - **didn't arrive**, for past months;
- **unplanned income**: money that arrived but didn't match anything you expected.

Olmenta matches incomes by who paid them, or by amount when there's no payer. If it didn't recognize one, use **This is…** on the unplanned income to say which expected income it was, or tell your coach ("ese ingreso de 2.080 es la nómina"). It remembers the payer for the next months.

When an income is late, your coach tells you, with **Add it** (if it arrived and you didn't record it) or **It's already here** (to link it).

## Future: the year ahead

**Future** shows the next 12 months if you keep your current plan:

- **Expected income, next 12 months.**
- **Planned costs**, split into payments, day-to-day and goals.
- **The gap**: what's left over or what's missing, per year and per month. If it's short, the plan needs adjusting, and it's better to know now than in March.

Below, each month has a row with its expected income, its payments, a "payments covered" or "short X €" tag, and two bars: how much of your day-to-day budget is funded, and how much is being set aside for later payments. Open a month to see every payment.

Each month's money goes first to that month's payments, then to day-to-day, then to setting aside for later payments and goals. So a short month shows up here months ahead.

## Your coach

Your coach keeps an eye on your plan and speaks up when something needs you. Its suggestions come in Home's month message: the most urgent one is **Do this first**, and the rest open from **more suggestions**, grouped by cause. You can also talk to it (see [Talking to your coach](#talking-to-your-coach)).

It suggests, from most to least urgent:

- **An income hasn't arrived** (more than 3 days late): add it, or link it if it's already recorded.
- **A debt payment is late**: see your debt plan.
- **A month ahead won't be fully covered**: see that month in Future, while there's still time to plan.
- **A category keeps going over** (2 of the last 3 months): **Raise it** to what you actually spend.
- **Money with no job**: give it one in the budget.
- **A category always has money left** (all of the last 3 months): **Lower it** and free that money for something else.

**Raise it** and **Lower it** change this month's amount in one tap, and the next months follow. If there isn't enough left to assign for a raise, the coach shows the categories you can take the money from. If your numbers changed since the suggestion appeared (say, you recorded another expense), nothing is applied: the coach shows the updated suggestion so you can check it first.

You see up to three groups at a time; **See more** shows the rest. **Not now** hides a suggestion for a week. If the situation is still there after that, it comes back.

When there's nothing to suggest, the coach says so: "No suggestions right now." That isn't a promise that your plan is fine; check Home's month status and Future for the full picture.

Your coach works out these suggestions from your own numbers, inside Olmenta. Nothing is sent anywhere to write them. (Answers to your questions do use an AI model; see [Talking to your coach](#talking-to-your-coach).)

## Credit cards

Olmenta handles cards so that paying them never surprises you:

- **A card purchase is spending at the moment you buy.** Groceries paid with the card come out of Groceries straight away, like cash.
- **The money moves to the card's payment category.** When the purchase was budgeted, that amount moves automatically to "Pago (your card)", ready for when the card is charged.
- **Paying the card is a transfer**, from your bank to the card. It doesn't count as spending again.
- **Spending more than a category has, on a card, becomes card debt.** It's not covered by the payment category and shows on the card as "X € not covered".

If your card already carried a balance when you started, that old balance is a debt. The [debt guide](debt-guide.md) explains how to give it a plan.

## Settings

- **Income**: your expected incomes (see [Income](#income)).
- **Categories**: create, rename, group and archive categories. Archived categories keep their history.
- **Accounts & cards**: a shortcut to Accounts.

## Plain-word glossary

- **Ready to assign**: money that has arrived and doesn't have a job yet.
- **Assigned**: what you gave a category this month.
- **Available**: what's left in a category to spend.
- **Carried over**: what stays in a category, or in Ready to assign, from last month.
- **Payment**: a cost with a date (rent, school, insurance), described once with its rule.
- **To be on time**: the monthly amount that catches you up when you're behind on a payment that's coming.
- **Covered / short**: whether the money set aside is enough for a payment.
- **Refund**: money back from a purchase. It returns to the purchase's category and isn't income.
- **Transfer**: money moving between your own accounts. It's never income or spending.
- **Preview**: what a change from the conversation would do, shown before anything changes.
- **Undo**: putting back exactly what a confirmed change replaced, while nothing it touched has changed since.
