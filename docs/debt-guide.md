# Managing your debts with Olmenta

Debt is where most people start, and it's the part that weighs the most. Olmenta gives you one honest picture of what you owe, tells you the order to pay it in, and shows the month you'll be free of it. No bank-speak: you won't find TAE, APR or amortization anywhere.

For the rest of the app, see the [user guide](user-guide.md).

## How Olmenta thinks about debt

Olmenta knows three kinds of debt:

| Kind | Examples | What you pay each month | When it ends |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A card** | the old balance on your Visa | a monthly plan you choose | when the balance reaches zero |
| **A loan with installments** | a personal loan, an iPhone, furniture bought in installments | the installment | after the last installment |
| **Money you owe someone** | a loan from family or a friend | whatever you can, or a set amount by a date | when it's paid back |

Some payments are **required** and some are **optional**:

- **Required:** the card plan, each loan installment, and money you owe someone by a date. They're part of your budget every month, like rent. If one is late, Olmenta reminds you.
- **Optional:** money you owe someone with no date, and any **extra** you decide to put toward debts.

## Where to find it

Open **What you owe** in the sidebar. On mobile, it's the button at the bottom of the home screen's side column.

The screen shows:

- **What you owe**: the total.
- **Debt-free in…**: the month you'll be done if you keep your plan.
- **Your debts in order**: the order to pay them in, with one sentence on why the first one goes first.
- **Extra for your debts each month**: optional (see [Paying faster with an extra](#paying-faster-with-an-extra)).

The **Work on my debts** button is always there: at the top of the screen, and on every debt. Use it any time you want to add a debt or change a plan.

## Adding a debt

Tap **Work on my debts**, then choose what it is.

### A card

Use this for a card that carries an old balance you're paying down.

1. **Which card?** Pick it from your cards. Olmenta shows what you owe on it, not counting the money already set aside for new purchases.
2. **Choose how to plan it.** Either way, Olmenta works out the other side for you:
   - **How much a month?** Type what you can pay, and it tells you "Done in January".
   - **When do you want to be done?** Pick a month, and it tells you how much to pay each month.
3. **What's the least the bank lets you pay?** Optional. If you know your minimum payment, enter it, and Olmenta warns you when your plan falls below it. Paying less than the minimum usually means a fee and more interest.
4. **How much does it charge you?** See [About interest](#about-interest).
5. **Add to my plan.**

From then on:

- the plan amount is set aside every month in the card's payment category, on top of the money for new purchases;
- the card payment shows in **Still to pay this month**, and counts as paid when you transfer the money to the card.

**What you owe on a card.** It's the card's balance minus the money already set aside for purchases you budgeted. This month's plan doesn't count until you actually transfer it: setting money aside isn't the same as paying.

### A loan with installments

1. **Name**: "Préstamo BBVA", "iPhone", "Ikea". If you already have a category with that name in your "Deudas" group, Olmenta uses it.
2. **Each installment**: the amount you pay each time.
3. **Installments left**: how many you still have to pay.
4. **Next one**: the month of the next installment.
5. **Day of the month** (optional): the day it's charged.

You don't need to know the exact balance. Olmenta works it out: installments left × installment. It also knows the month of the last one.

The installment is assigned automatically every month in its category, shows in **Still to pay this month**, and counts as paid when the expense is recorded (typed or imported).

### Money you owe someone

1. **Name**: for example, "Jose y Ruby".
2. **Who lent it to you?** Optional.
3. **How much do you owe?**
4. **Do they need it back by a date?**
   - **Yes:** pick the month. Olmenta sets aside a bit every month until then, so the money is ready on time. It becomes a required payment.
   - **No:** it's paid whenever you can, usually with the extra once the other debts are done.

Every payment you record in that category lowers what you owe.

## About interest

The question **How much does it charge you?** has three answers: "x % a month", "x % a year", or "I don't know". Your card statement usually says the monthly rate; loan contracts often say the yearly one.

The rate is optional, and Olmenta only uses it for two things:

- **Ordering your debts.** The one that charges you the most goes first, because every euro you put there saves the most.
- **Telling you what it costs.** One sentence, like "~9 €/month just for owing it".

If you don't know a rate, choose "I don't know". You can add it later.

## The order to pay in

Olmenta puts your debts in this order:

1. **Debts with a known rate**, the most expensive first.
2. **Debts whose rate you don't know.**
3. **Money you owe someone without interest**, last.

When two debts are tied, the one with less left goes first: it's the closest to done.

This only matters for the **extra money**. The required payments (the card plan, the installments) are paid every month no matter the order.

## Paying faster with an extra

At the bottom of What you owe, **Extra for your debts each month** lets you say how much more you can put toward debts. It's optional.

- **All the extra goes to the first debt in the list.** That's where it saves you the most.
- **When that debt is paid off, its money moves to the next one.** If you were paying 200 € a month on the card plus 100 € extra, from the month after it's done, 300 € a month go to the next debt. Each debt you finish makes the next one go faster.
- **The debt-free month updates** as you change the extra.

### First, a small cushion

If you add an extra but have little saved for surprises, Olmenta suggests keeping a small cushion first (between 300 € and 1.000 €, depending on your monthly debt payments). The reason: without it, the next unexpected bill ends up back on the card, and you lose the ground you gained.

It's a suggestion, never a block. You can set the extra anyway.

## Don't forget an installment

Debt payments are part of your month:

- **Budget:** they're assigned automatically in their categories.
- **Home screen:** they show in **Still to pay this month**, with "covered" or "short X €".
- **Late payments:** when a payment is more than 3 days past its day and hasn't gone out, your coach tells you: "Your Ikea payment is late. It was due on the 5th and hasn't gone out yet." **See my plan** takes you to What you owe; **Not now** hides it for a week.

## A loan that was set up as a card

If you created a loan as a card account (it happens; it's how many people start), the app treats it like a card with a huge unpaid balance. Fix it in two steps:

1. In **Accounts**, open the account's menu (**⋯**) and choose **This is a loan, not a card**.
2. Enter the installment, the installments left, the next one and the day, then **Move to What you owe**.

Olmenta then:

- creates the loan in What you owe, in the "Deudas" group;
- moves any money you had set aside for it into the loan's category;
- archives the old account. Its history stays.

## Changing a plan

Tap **Work on my debts** on the debt you want to change. You can change the plan, the minimum, the installments left, the amount owed, the date or the rate. **Remove this debt** deletes the plan; the category and its history stay.

If you pay extra on a loan with installments, ask your bank how many installments are left and update **Installments left**.

Payments that belong to a debt can't be edited from the category's payment editor. That keeps the order and the dates right. The editor shows "Managed from What you owe" with a link.

## When a debt is paid off

It shows as paid off, with a short celebration line, and offers **Remove the plan**. Once every debt is paid off, What you owe says so. Your card stops being a debt: from then on you pay it in full every month and it costs you nothing.

## A worked example

You owe:

- 634,62 € on your Visa, at 1,5 % a month;
- an Ikea loan with 10 installments of 45 €;
- 2.900 € to your in-laws, with no date.

1. **Add the Visa with "How much a month?" = 200 €.** Olmenta says "Done in January" and "~9,52 €/month just for owing it".
2. **Add the Ikea loan.** It's required every month and ends after the 10th installment.
3. **Add your in-laws' debt with no date.** It goes last in the list.
4. **Set an extra of 100 €.** The order is Visa, Ikea, in-laws:
   - the Visa gets 200 € + 100 € and finishes a month earlier;
   - from then on, those 300 € a month go to the next debt;
   - the debt-free month shows when your in-laws are fully paid back.

## Questions

**Do I have to enter the rate?**
No. Without it, Olmenta orders by what's left to pay and doesn't show the interest sentence.

**Why didn't my card debt go down when I assigned the money?**
Setting money aside isn't paying. It goes down when you transfer the money to the card.

**My debt is in another currency.**
Record it in euros at today's rate. Olmenta works in euros.

**Can I put a family debt first, even without interest?**
Not yet: the order goes by rate. If the lender needs it back by a date, give it that date. It becomes a required payment, and the money is set aside on time.
